How to read these numbers

Wide-spacing third-order dynamic range (10 MHz spacing) shows how well the radio resists intermodulation from strong signals far away, such as pagers, broadcast towers and mountaintop sites. This is the number that matters most in cities.

Narrow-spacing third-order dynamic range uses a 20 kHz spacing, the same idea for nearby channels. Adjacent rejection is how well the radio ignores a strong signal on the next channel (20 kHz). Sensitivity is the signal for 12 dB SINAD, so lower is better.

Score is wide + narrow + adjacent, in dB, for the chosen band. Older radios were never tested at wide spacing, so their score (marked †) is a partial sum and understates them. Use Complete data to compare like with like.

Data verification

Cross-check. Every row of the supplied file was compared with the QRP Blog compilation of QST lab results (94 radios). 93 matched number for number. One did not: the Kenwood TH-D74A has a 2 m wide-spacing figure of 87 dB in the supplied file. That is the Band B (wideband receiver) result. The printed QST review (April 2017) gives 79 dB for Band A, the VHF/UHF receiver, and its Key Measurements Summary is explicitly Band A, so this page uses 79 dB. The TH-D75A review (QST September 2024) shows the same pattern: Band B measures 79 dB and Band A 77 dB at 146 MHz. Band B edging ahead of Band A on 2 m in both radios is a consistent trait of the design, not a transposition. A successor that lost 10 dB of wide-spacing range while gaining elsewhere would also be implausible. The TH-D75A row was supplied with Band B figures and now shows Band A, so the two radios are compared on the same basis.

Checked against the ARRL reviews: 14 radios (TH-D74A, TH-D75A, FT-5DR, FT-3DR, FT-818, IC-705, AT-D878UV, ID-52A, FT-60R, FT-70DR and the four added radios). The other 91 agree with the QRP Blog table, which means the numbers match that compilation, not that I traced each one to its original QST issue. No radio is left without a check. The IC-705 (QST, February 2021), FT-3DR (April 2020), FT-818ND (January 2019) and AT-D878UV (July 2019) rows match their reviews exactly.

Mode and receiver choices. The FT-5DR figures are Receiver B, as in the ARRL summary; its Receiver A is noticeably weaker (62 / 73 / 66 dB on 2 m). The ID-52A figures match the ARRL summary but use FM-N (narrow) results; its FM results are 85 / 71 / 78 dB on 2 m and 77 / 74 / 72 dB on 70 cm, so it is not on exactly the same basis as most rows.

Outliers. The IC-T7A (105 dB), IC-W32A (91), DJ-G5TH (90) and FT-50R (90) have 70 cm wide-spacing values far above their other results. I could not trace them, so they carry a ⚠. The FT-70DR's 84 dB on 70 cm is confirmed by its review, so a high value alone is not proof of an error.

Method and caveats

The original table was supplied as a file and follows the layout of the QRP Blog compilation of QST lab results. Its Total Rejection column equals the 2 m sum of the three dB figures, which this page recomputes for each band.

Added rows are single-unit ARRL Lab results read from the published QST reviews, FM mode, receiver A where a radio has two. Mobiles, base stations and wideband scanners were left out as a different category. The ARRL also publishes a members-only comparison tool at compare.arrl.org with newer entries that I could not open.
